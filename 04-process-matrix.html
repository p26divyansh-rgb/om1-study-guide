4 Product/Service–Process Matrix

Topic 4 of 12 · Job shop, batch, mass and continuous processes; the Service-Process Matrix.

The Product-Process Matrix plots process choice (vertical) against volume & standardization (horizontal). As volume rises and products standardize, firms move down the diagonal toward more efficient, less flexible processes.

Process typeVolumeVarietyExample
Job ShopVery lowHighly customized, one-offCustom-made furniture workshop
Batch ProductionLow–mediumProducts made in discrete batches/groupsBakery: 100 chocolate cakes, changeover, 100 vanilla cakes
Mass Production (Assembly Line)HighLarge volume of one standardized productCar assembly line
Continuous ProcessVery high, 24/7Little to no varietyOil refinery, cement plant

Service-Process Matrix (Schmenner)

Analogous matrix for services, axes = degree of labor intensity vs. degree of customer interaction/customization.

Low customer interactionHigh customer interaction
Low labor intensityService Factory (airlines, trucking)Service Shop (hospitals, auto repair)
High labor intensityMass Service (retail, schools)Professional Service (doctors, lawyers, consultants)
Challenges shift by quadrant: high-interaction services struggle with quality-control consistency and demand management; high-labor-intensity services struggle with hiring/training/scheduling large workforces.
