Types of Layout

LayoutDescriptionExample
Product LayoutEquipment arranged in the exact sequence of production steps; product moves station to stationCar assembly line
Process LayoutSimilar machines/functions grouped together (departments); products/patients move between departments as neededHospital, machine shop
Cellular LayoutDifferent machine types grouped into a "cell" that produces a family of similar productsFurniture manufacturing cell
Fixed-Position LayoutThe product stays in one place; workers, equipment & materials come to itShip, aircraft, building construction

Labor Specialization on the Assembly Line

Advantages
Management: rapid training, easy recruiting, high output from simple repetitive work, close control of workflow.
Labor: little education needed, easy to learn the job.
Disadvantages
Management: reduced improvement (limited worker perspective), low flexibility to change the line, difficulty controlling quality.
Labor: boredom, little control over pace → fatigue/frustration, little chance to advance.

Spatial Configurations

Straight Line Design vs. U-Shaped Design. U-shaped lines let one worker handle stations at both the start and end of the line, improve visibility/communication between workers, and make it easier to rebalance the line when demand (and hence required staffing) changes.

Assembly Line Balancing — the 8-Step Procedure Must know

  1. Draw the precedence diagram (tasks + predecessor relationships).
  2. Determine required workstation cycle time = Production time available per day / Required output per day.
  3. Determine theoretical minimum number of workstations = (Sum of all task times) / Cycle time.
  4. Select an assignment rule (e.g., longest task time first, most followers first).
  5. Assign tasks one at a time to the first workstation until the sum of task times equals the cycle time, or no other task is feasible (predecessor/time constraint).
  6. Repeat step 5 for the next workstation until all tasks are assigned.
  7. Compute line efficiency.
  8. If efficiency is unsatisfactory, rebalance using a different rule.
Line Balancing Formulas Cycle Time (CT) = Production time available per day / Required units per day Theoretical Min. Workstations (N) = ΣTask times / Cycle Time (round UP) Line Efficiency = ΣTask times / (Actual no. of workstations × Cycle Time) Balance Delay = 1 − Efficiency Labour Content = Sum of all labour (task) times to make one unit Available Labour Time = Number of workers × Cycle Time Labour Utilization = Labour Content / Available Labour Time
Zoning & personnel constraints (as tested in the 2025 "Artisan Shirt" question): a single workstation often cannot mix tasks from different process zones (e.g., Cutting/Stitching/Finishing), and a workstation's skill requirement = the highest-skilled task assigned to it (a senior worker CAN do junior tasks, not vice-versa). Always re-verify max daily output, labor cost/unit, and any KPI after re-balancing.

Process Batch vs. Transfer Batch

  • Process Batch: number of units processed together at one stage before changing over to a different product/batch.
  • Transfer Batch: number of units physically moved together from one stage to the next (can be smaller than the process batch — moving in smaller transfer batches overlaps stages and reduces flow time, even though process batch/capacity stays the same).
