10 Bullwhip Effect & Supply Chain Guaranteed MCQ topic

Topic 10 of 12 · Causes, symptoms and remedies of the bullwhip effect, plus the Beer Game takeaways.

Bullwhip Effect: a small change in actual end-customer demand causes increasingly amplified swings in orders as you move upstream through the supply chain (Customer → Retailer → Distributor → Manufacturer → Supplier). Classic demonstration: the Beer Game.

Numeric feel for it

Customer demand rises 100 → 110 (only +10%). Retailer, fearing more growth, orders 125. Distributor sees retailer's jump and orders 150. Manufacturer sees that and produces 180. A tiny real signal becomes a huge upstream swing.

Symptoms

  • Excessive inventory ("just in case" buffers)
  • Poor demand forecasts
  • Insufficient capacity when a surge hits
  • Long backlogs

Causes (know these — classic MCQ list)

  1. Demand forecast updating — each stage forecasts off the orders it receives, not real customer sales, compounding errors.
  2. Order batching — infrequent large orders (0-0-0-400) instead of steady small ones (100-100-100-100) make demand look spikier than it is.
  3. Price fluctuations / promotions — discounts cause forward-buying, misread as a genuine demand increase, followed by a post-promotion crash.
  4. Lack of information sharing — no visibility into true end-customer demand.
  5. Long lead times / too many intermediaries — long lead times push firms to over-order "just in case"; every extra intermediary adds distortion.
  6. Disorganization / lack of coordination — each firm reacts independently to demand changes.
  7. Free returns / order cancellation — firms over-order, planning to cancel/return the excess, creating phantom demand spikes.

Remedies

  1. Use actual point-of-sale data instead of each tier re-forecasting independently.
  2. Break large batches into smaller, more frequent orders.
  3. Stabilize prices (Every-Day-Low-Price over frequent promotions).
  4. Improve information sharing across the chain (sales, inventory, forecasts, production plans).
  5. Respond to demand changes proportionately, not dramatically.
The core Beer Game/exam lesson: mitigating the bullwhip effect is fundamentally about improving information sharing and collaboration across the whole chain so every tier can see real end-customer demand — not about ordering in isolation or stockpiling more inventory.

Pillars of Supply-Chain Coordination

Trust · Coordination · Partnership · Integration — the antidote to the bullwhip effect is treating the supply chain as one coordinated system (as in Toyota's Keiretsu model) rather than a sequence of isolated, self-interested links.
